The three problems

parser: the shared parser reads dates month first. All tenants on file write the day first. Any date with a day of 12 or less comes out with day and month swapped: booked 7 April, invite for 4 July. These reports came in by app or email from any tenant, or through the booking-v2 form. That form’s hint says DD/MM, so people type the date correctly and the parser then flips it.

hint: booking-v3 tells people to type the month first. Its date field prints MM/DD/YYYY. People follow the hint, and the form reads what they typed the tenant’s way, day first. The swap happens in the form, before the parser. Only form reports from booking-v3 tenants have this problem. They show the same symptom as parser, but the bug is in a different place and needs a different fix: change the hint, or the way the form reads it.

shift: the date moves by one day. These reports show no swap. The date lands one day early or late, and which day depends on where the reader is. One example: “Client in Lisbon got Tuesday. Client in Toronto got Monday.” This looks like a time-zone conversion of the booking date. The desk already listed these reports as a one-day move in desk-notes.json, and it counts them under parser because of their tenant. The parser fix will not change them, so they need their own ticket too.

How a report is counted

The desk’s own view, as exported (causes.ts, the same rule as the cause table on #4471):

  1. Count each report id once.
  2. The tenant decides, not intake’s tag (a guess from the reporter’s browser): a form report from a booking-v3 tenant is hint, and every other report from a day-first tenant is parser.
  3. A report with no tenant on file is unplaced: read it by hand.
  4. A report the desk read as a one-day move (desk-notes.json) is also counted as shift. It stays counted under its tenant’s cause as well, where the desk files it.

The table runs this on every build (#lib/kb/problems), so new reports in the export are counted without anyone editing this page.

Lessons

  • Do not merge reports because the symptom is the same. “Wrong day” came from three places.
  • Read the bodies before you call a ticket known.
